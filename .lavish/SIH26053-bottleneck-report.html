Core mechanism

Why errors enter during 3D to 2.5D conversion

Irreversible loss: one stored height cannot encode road under bridge, curb face, tree canopy, or a pedestrian above the same XY footprint.
Conditional loss: aggregation policy decides whether a pothole, curb, overhang, or obstacle disappears.
Accumulating error: timestamp, pose, calibration, and resampling errors compound when scans are fused.
Prioritized inventory

15 current bottlenecks

P0 items can create unsafe geometry or systematic map misalignment. P1 items usually determine whether the system remains accurate and real time outside a demo.

P001

Single-surface representation loss

A standard elevation cell assumes z = f(x,y). Bridges, underpasses, vegetation canopies, vertical walls, and stacked returns violate that assumption. No aggregation rule can reconstruct data that was never retained.

Failure: false free space, missing overhead obstacle, or phantom ground.

P002

Many-to-one cell collisions

Multiple points with different heights, labels, intensities, and motion states map to one XY cell. Keeping nearest, highest, lowest, mean, or last-written point each destroys a different class of evidence.

Failure: max height hides potholes, min height hides overhangs, mean creates a surface that never existed, last-write creates nondeterminism.

P003

Resolution-boundary seams

If 5 cm and 50 cm zones use independent origins or non-integer ratios, cells overlap or leave gaps. Even with aligned power-of-two levels, neighborhood operations encounter T-junctions and unequal support areas.

Failure: cracks, duplicated obstacles, discontinuous slope, and planner cost jumps at zone edges.

P004

Unstable coordinate indexing

Floating-point division plus floor/round, negative coordinates, changing map origins, and inconsistent closed intervals can assign the same world point to different cells across frames or modules.

Failure: one-cell jitter, double insertion on borders, holes, and non-reproducible maps.

P005

Intra-scan motion distortion

A spinning LiDAR does not capture all points at one instant. Projecting a whole sweep with one pose bends walls and curbs before gridding. Deskew requires per-point time plus a sufficiently accurate continuous pose estimate.

Failure: smeared edges and systematic misalignment that resembles poor calibration.

P006

Pose, clock, and extrinsic calibration error

LiDAR-to-IMU extrinsics, timestamp offsets, roll/pitch error, vertical drift, and scan-matching drift move points before fusion. Angular error becomes larger lateral displacement with range.

Failure: doubled curbs, wavy roads, thick walls, and long-range cell shifts.

P007

Unknown space confused with free space

Far cells are sparse because angular sampling spreads with range; occlusion, grazing angles, dark/reflective materials, rain, and limited vertical channels create missing returns. Empty cells are not evidence of traversability.

Failure: unsafe free-space inference or over-conservative unexplored regions.

P008

Dynamic-object ghosts and state oscillation

Moving points corrupt registration and leave trails when fused into the static layer. A temporarily stopped vehicle can be classified as static; removing whole detections can also delete nearby permanent structure.

Failure: false obstacles, damaged background, unstable localization, and motion-label flicker.

P109

Cross-resolution fusion is not neutral

A 50 cm parent summarizes 100 cells at 5 cm. Height mean, max, variance, occupancy, semantic probability, and dynamic probability require different reduction rules. Most are not reversible, and repeated downsample-upsample cycles amplify bias.

Failure: semantic bleeding, underestimated uncertainty, lost curbs and potholes.

P110

Uncertainty is correlated and range dependent

Sensor noise, pose uncertainty, and resampling affect many cells together, but simple per-cell Kalman updates often treat measurements as independent. Coarse cells receive more points and can become falsely overconfident.

Failure: confidence values that look precise but do not bound the real height error.

P111

Feature scale changes with cell size

Curbs, potholes, poles, and feet can be smaller than a far cell. Surface normals, roughness, connected components, and slope thresholds behave differently when their metric support changes across levels.

Failure: the same object changes class or disappears only because it crossed a resolution ring.

P112

Semantic and geometry layers age differently

Elevation, class probability, occupancy, instance ID, velocity, visibility, and timestamp may update on different schedules. Regridding one layer without all validity masks creates internally inconsistent cells.

Failure: a fresh height paired with a stale label or velocity.

P1 compute13

Adaptive structures fight regular compute

Quadtrees and per-cell lists save empty space but introduce tree walks, dynamic allocation, irregular neighbors, poor cache locality, and GPU branch divergence. Polar grids need seam-aware convolutions. Standard CNNs and costmaps expect regular arrays.

Failure: memory savings do not translate into latency savings.

P1 compute14

The moving fovea causes map churn

As the vehicle moves, world regions repeatedly enter and leave fine resolution. Naive reallocation or resampling makes features flicker, discards history, and causes latency spikes exactly near safety boundaries.

Failure: temporal instability and unpredictable frame time.

P1 validation15

Benchmarks hide distance and topology failures

Global mIoU, mean height RMSE, and average FPS can hide small near-field hazards, far-field class collapse, boundary cracks, temporal ghosts, and missed overhangs. Public labels also rarely provide synchronized multi-surface elevation ground truth.

Failure: a system passes aggregate metrics but fails the safety cases in this statement.

Representation choice

Data structures and their bottlenecks

No structure wins every axis. For a navigation GIS, the internal map must serve perception and planning while still publishing stable, georeferenced, queryable spatial layers.

Algorithm audit

3D to adaptive 2.5D conversion pipeline

Each stage needs an explicit contract. Skipping any contract converts an upstream error into a permanent map artifact.

1. Timestamp, synchronize, and calibrate

Retain per-point acquisition time, LiDAR ring/channel, sensor frame, transform version, and calibration version. Reject scans without a valid time-aligned pose interval.

2. Deskew in continuous time

Transform every point to a common scan time using IMU/LiDAR odometry. Do not apply one rigid transform to a moving-platform sweep.

3. Transform to one gravity-aligned frame

Use one explicit frame convention and propagate pose covariance. Delay map-relative quantization until after all transforms.

4. Assign semantic and motion evidence per point

Keep class probabilities and motion probability, not only hard labels. Treat potentially dynamic objects separately from confirmed moving objects.

5. Select level from policy, not point density alone

Distance can choose a target level, but uncertainty, predicted stopping corridor, object boundary, and hazard salience may require refinement. Add hysteresis so cells do not oscillate between levels.

6. Convert world coordinates to stable integer keys

Define a global origin, integer scale, half-open cell bounds, negative-coordinate behavior, and parent-child key mapping. Test points exactly on every boundary.

7. Accumulate before reducing

Group all points per cell, then compute count, min/max height, robust location, variance, label histogram, timestamps, and source IDs. Never rely on write order.

8. Detect multi-surface ambiguity

Split vertical modes when height gaps exceed uncertainty-aware thresholds. Mark unresolved cells as ambiguous rather than forcing a ground or obstacle height.

9. Fuse through time with visibility and dynamics

Use uncertainty-weighted updates, ray visibility, dynamic gating, and decay. Store last-observed time and observation count. Never convert unobserved to free without ray evidence.

10. Resolve level transitions once

Define authoritative ownership at boundaries, conservative parent aggregation, fine-cell halos, and one-way publish rules. Avoid repeated upsample/downsample cycles.

11. Publish synchronized navigation GIS layers

Publish elevation, uncertainty, semantics, dynamics, traversability, routing cost, validity, resolution, and timestamp as one versioned snapshot. Every tile needs a coordinate reference system, world bounds, lineage, and metric cell extent so GIS queries and route planning cannot mix incompatible states.

12. Evaluate perception and navigation GIS outcomes

Report near/far mIoU, height RMSE by range, boundary continuity, overhang recall, curb/pothole recall, dynamic ghost persistence, route-cost stability, topology correctness, GIS tile freshness, memory, p95/p99 latency, and deterministic replay.

Tests that should fail a design

Minimum adversarial cases

  • Bridge over drivable road in the same XY footprint
  • 5 to 15 cm curb and pothole straddling a level boundary
  • Wall exactly on negative-coordinate and tile boundaries
  • Vehicle that moves, stops briefly, then moves again
  • Fast turn during a spinning-LiDAR sweep
  • Same bag replayed with different thread schedules
  • Far pedestrian partially occluded by a pole
  • Map origin relocation while semantic output is delayed
Acceptance evidence

Metrics beyond accuracy and FPS

  • Cell coverage has no gaps or overlaps at every level
  • Round-trip key mapping is exact at boundaries
  • Height discontinuity at scale seams stays within tolerance
  • Unknown, free, and occupied remain distinct
  • Dynamic ghost lifetime is bounded in seconds and meters
  • Near-field hazards receive safety-weighted recall
  • Routing topology and costs stay stable across GIS tile boundaries
  • p95 and p99 sensor-to-GIS latency meet the navigation budget
  • Memory includes allocator, indexing, layer, and visualization overhead
Evidence base

Primary and implementation sources

The list combines established representation limits with recent work on projection loss, dynamic scenes, and multi-resolution processing. Engineering conclusions about seams, integer indexing, and synchronized layers are derived from these mechanisms.

  1. Triebel, Pfaff, Burgard - Multi-Level Surface Maps: standard elevation maps cannot represent vertical structures or multiple levels.
  2. Fankhauser et al. - Probabilistic Terrain Mapping: pose drift, sensor noise, and confidence bounds in grid elevation mapping.
  3. Kaufmann et al. - Multi-Resolution Elevation Mapping: fixed-topology pyramid levels, probabilistic fusion, and hazard detection degradation versus cell size and distance.
  4. FLARES: explicit many-to-one information loss when several 3D points map into a 2D grid.
  5. PolarNet: uneven LiDAR point distribution, polar partitioning, seam-aware convolution, and accuracy drop with distance.
  6. LOAM: rolling acquisition, motion distortion, registration, and odometry drift.
  7. LIO-SAM: IMU preintegration for point-cloud deskew and real-time map building.
  8. DGMSE, 2026: dynamic-object ghosting, state oscillation, incomplete removal, and real-time tradeoffs.
  9. Accelerating Probabilistic Volumetric Mapping: OctoMap's ray-shooting bottleneck.
  10. ANYbotics Grid Map: multi-layer 2.5D arrays and circular-buffer map repositioning.
  11. ANYbotics Elevation Mapping: uncertainty-aware fusion parameters, visibility cleanup, and dynamic-environment tradeoffs.
  12. S3Net: projection methods lose 3D topology, while sparse 3D methods trade accuracy for compute.
